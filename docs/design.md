# Design decisions

## Why one seat per person

The office has three people with distinct portfolios. A shared dashboard
that shows everything to everyone is a dashboard nobody trusts. Role-based
views are how you get people to actually use it.

The seats are not a permission system in the security sense. They are the
office structure written down.

## Why the handbook is the product

The office's real work is the handbook. Everything else — dashboards,
forecasts, trackers — is in service of executing it well.

## Why provenance is non-negotiable

A sample figure next to a real one, without a label, is a figure that will
eventually mislead someone. The provenance discipline is the single most
important design decision in the build.

## Why gaps are named

A Dean acting on invented course data is worse than a Dean asking the
Registrar for the real feed. Naming gaps is a finding, not a failure.

## Why the control tower is ordered as it is

Check first, then decide. Urgency, then RACI, then operation, then
cadence. The order is the point.

## Why the dashboard answers five questions

Where does the faculty stand? Where is the ladder heading? Can we teach
the curriculum? Is the academic year on track? What could go wrong, and
what's waiting on me?

A Dean opens one page and gets one picture. That page should not be a
report. It should be a briefing.
